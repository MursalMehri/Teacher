A Letter of Gratitude

Dear Teacher Sudaba & Teacher Somayeh,

There is a quiet truth that students rarely say out loud: a teacher is never just a person who explains lessons. You are the voice a student hears later, in some future classroom or difficult moment, saying “you can try again.”

You took things that felt too large — a hard topic, a shaky confidence, a fear of raising our hands — and you made them small enough to hold. You repeated explanations without sighing. You noticed when we were quiet for the wrong reasons. You celebrated answers that were almost right, because you knew almost is where learning actually lives.

We remember more than the curriculum. We remember the patience in your voice, the way a lesson could turn into a story, the way you made every student feel like the smartest person in the room for exactly as long as they needed to. Those moments didn't feel important while they were happening — but they were building something. They were building us.

Education changes the direction of a life, and you two were part of the turn. Somewhere in every good habit we have, in every problem we don't give up on, there is a piece of your time, your effort, your care. None of it was wasted. All of it counted.

So thank you — for the lessons, for the patience, for the belief that was sometimes louder than our own. A student may forget a formula, but never the teacher who made them feel capable of learning it.

With gratitude, always,
Your Students

A Little Secret

There is one message we never said out loud in class.

We noticed everything — the extra minutes, the repeated explanations, the way you asked how we were doing, not just how we were studying. You may have thought it was just your job. It wasn't. It was a gift, and we kept every piece of it.
